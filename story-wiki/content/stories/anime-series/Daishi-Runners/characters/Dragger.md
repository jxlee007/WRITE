# Dragger

**Appears In:** [[Daishi-Runners]]
**Role:** Antagonist — Tragic / Cursed Swordsman
**Inspired By:** Deker (PRS) — structurally, origin is entirely original

---

## Core Traits

- Half-human, half-demon hybrid — caught between two worlds
- Defined by a **curse of endless battle hunger**: cannot find peace until the Ultimate Duel is fought
- No loyalty to the demon hierarchy — operates entirely on his own terms
- Outwardly cold, inwardly suffering
- The most skilled individual swordsman in the story's world
- Carries one single fragment of the human world: **the sound of jingling bells from an earring** — heard in the last second before he vanished. No face, no name. Just the sound. He does not know what it means. He only knows something was there.

---

## The Clan Rule — Why He Was Rejected

The Daishi order has held a **centuries-old closed-door policy**: no outsiders. Ever.

Their reasoning: if the Disk power falls into the wrong hands, it could cause catastrophic events. They are the self-appointed gatekeepers. Birth into the clan — or formal sponsorship — is the only accepted path.

They also held an unspoken assumption: that an unsponsored outsider **could not** summon a Disk without their guidance. Not dangerous, just impossible. A theoretical curiosity, dismissed with something close to amusement.

> They were wrong. And their dismissal — the arrogance behind it — is part of what set the catastrophe in motion.

## Origin — The Night of the Ritual

### Before That Night

Dragger was an outsider aspirant. Not from the clan. No sponsor. No master.

What he had was **dedication** — witnessed, ironically, by the one person inside the clan walls who was paying attention: **[[Draggers-Love-Interest]]**, the daughter of a clan elder. She watched him train, study, and pursue the art with an intensity that no one inside the order matched. She fell in love with what she saw.

She had decided to confess to him. She was going to find him outside the Daishi quarters and tell him she had chosen him.

She never got the chance.

### The Clan's Countermeasure

When the clan formally rejected Dragger's petition, they did not simply turn him away. They had him **tailed by clan spies** — runners ordered to shadow him and report.

When the spies confirmed he was attempting the ritual alone, the clan deployed a countermeasure:

**The Evil-Lure Sticker** — a seal infused with dormant evil energy, pasted onto a tree near Dragger's dwelling. Its properties:
- Inert under normal conditions — undetectable, harmless
- **Activates only during high-energy events** — specifically drawn to and amplified by strong spiritual energy
- During a Disk summoning ritual, it would function as a beacon: pulling ambient evil energy toward the site, overwhelming an unshielded practitioner

Their intent was not to kill him — it was to **ensure he failed**, to confirm their assumption, and to send a message. The sticker would ruin the ritual. He would walk away humiliated and the matter would be closed.

They did not understand what they had built. A trap that activates at peak spiritual energy, placed next to a man with genuine emotional resonance strong enough to actually summon a Disk.

### What Happened Inside

Dragger began the ritual. His emotional key: **loyalty**.

The Disk responded. A white light began to grow through the dwelling — pure, legitimate, real.

The sticker activated.

The white energy — a high-energy event, exactly the trigger condition — drew the corrupted seal to life. Evil energy flooded in. The light began to shift.

**White → Red.**

By the time the red took hold, Dragger had been pulled across — delivered, disoriented and half-formed, before the Top Dog (the main demon villain). He was no longer bound to the human world. He simply **vanished**.

### What She Found

The clan daughter had learned of the sticker. She ran.

She arrived crying — she'd been running hard, driven by panic and the confession she hadn't yet made — and found:

- Shattered windows
- An empty dwelling
- **A burnt tree** — the sticker had fired

No Dragger. No body. No proof of anything.

She knew something had happened. She could not prove what. The clan, when pressed, would have plausible deniability: *we placed a protective measure; he attempted an illegal ritual alone; we cannot be responsible for whatever occurred.*

She was left with a burnt tree, a silent house, and a confession she never delivered.

### The Clan's Reckoning

They had spent centuries preventing a catastrophic event by keeping outsiders out.

Their own countermeasure — deployed against an outsider they dismissed — **caused the catastrophic event.**

> The irony is the thematic core: the clan's fear of uncontrolled power led them to take an action that produced exactly the outcome they feared. Gatekeeping did not protect the world. It created Dragger.

---

### The Mental Trap (Inside the Demon Realm)

The Top Dog did not destroy him. He read the situation precisely — a disoriented outsider, mid-ritual, who had just proven his own worth and had it immediately stolen — and laid a psychological trap:

| Option | Cost |
|--------|------|
| **Duel the Top Dog** | Defeat = lose the Disk forever |
| **Accept the bound evil katana** | Keep the Disk — but the sword binds him |

Dragger chose the katana. He kept the Disk.

The Disk is now locked — real and legitimate, earned through genuine emotional resonance — but tainted and inaccessible. The bound sword is his curse. The Top Dog weaponised the one wound that was already open: *you were right all along, and no one helped you.*

## Arc

```
Curse-bound wanderer, no longer human-world-bound
    → fixates on the strongest Daishi Runner as his Ultimate Duel target
    → defies the demon hierarchy when it conflicts with the duel obsession
    → fragments of the past begin to surface — a woman running, a light turning red
    → final duel — the protagonist Runner recognises his Disk as legitimate
    → curse breaks, human soul freed in death
```

- His redemption is **not survival** — it is peace
- The moment the protagonist sees his Disk for what it is changes the meaning of the final fight
