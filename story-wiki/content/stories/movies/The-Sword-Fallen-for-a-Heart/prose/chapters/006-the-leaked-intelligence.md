## 📜 Chapter Prose

The secret chamber beneath the citadel was cold and dry, smelling of old parchment and charcoal. 

A hooded operative stood before the Rajmata, holding a blank wax tablet. 

"The Southern Ally has received the cipher," the operative whispered, his voice barely audible above the candle flame's hiss. "They have verified the weakness in the Eastern Kingdom's northern garrison. Their vanguard marches at midnight."

The Rajmata stood motionless in the dim light. Her fingers did not shake as she pressed her seal into the wax, sealing the fate of a sovereign realm.

"Give them the information," she said softly, her eyes fixed on the flickering flame. "Let them move. My son will understand I was right to keep him home."

---

*Three days later.*

The iron doors of the throne room slammed open. 

The Prince strode down the central aisle, his war cloak billowing behind him, his hand gripping the hilt of his broadsword. Black smoke was visible on the eastern horizon through the high clerestory windows.

"The Political Ally has invaded the Eastern realm!" he roared, halting before the raised dais where the Rajmata sat in state. "They are burning the border towns! I am assembling four regiments to march to her defense!"

The Rajmata did not rise. She looked down at him from the stone throne, her face an unyielding mask of regal authority.

"You will assemble no regiments," she commanded, her voice ringing across the vast stone hall.

The Prince froze, his eyes wide with shock. "Mother! They will tear her kingdom apart! She has no garrison capable of holding that mountain pass!"

"And if you march into that pass," the Rajmata replied, leaning forward, her voice dropping into a razor-sharp whisper that carried only to his ears, "you walk into a trap designed to draw our crown into total annihilation. The stability of this throne rests on your presence here."

"I am the King!" he shouted, his knuckles turning white on his sword hilt. "I do not leave allies to burn!"

"You are the King of *this* realm!" she thundered, standing upright, her presence filling the vast chamber. "Your duty is here. You will stay."

The silence that followed was suffocating. The Prince stood in the center of the hall, his breath ragged, his chest heaving. He looked at the elders of the council standing along the walls—every one of them silent, nodding in passive agreement with the Rajmata's command.

Slowly, agonizingly, his hand loosened from his sword hilt. He stepped back.

He obeyed.
