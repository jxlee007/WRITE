# Chapter 004 — The Unspoken Warning

**Location:** Private Audience Chamber, Royal Palace  
**Characters:** The Prince, The Rajmata  
**Purpose:** Show the confrontation where the Prince announces his intention to visit the Princess, and the Rajmata delivers her silent warning, establishing his fatal flaw of dismissing her statecraft.  
**Structure:** Proactive Scene (Goal: Prince seeks mother's blessing for journey $\rightarrow$ Conflict: Rajmata's cryptic warning of unseen enemies $\rightarrow$ Disaster: Prince dismisses her warning as controlling paranoia).

## 📜 Chapter Prose

The morning sun cast long, golden bars through the tall arched windows of the council chamber. Rain from the night before dripped rhythmically from the stone eaves outside.

The Rajmata stood at the map table, her hand resting over a rolled leather map of the eastern frontier. She did not look up as the heavy doors clicked closed behind her son.

"The border lords report your horses are saddled," she said, her tone smooth and level as beaten copper.

The Prince stepped to the edge of the table. He stood taller now than when he had left for Gurukul, carrying the broad shoulders of a king who had led three successful campaigns without losing a single outpost.

"I ride to the Eastern Court," he said, fixing his gaze on her. "The girl from the riverbank is their princess. There is no barrier of caste, nor conflict of interest. It is a sovereign match."

The Rajmata slowly turned her head. Her dark eyes, framed by faint lines of decades of silent statecraft, searched his face. 

"You see a crown and a memory," she said quietly. "You do not see the land beneath her feet."

A brief shadow of irritation crossed his brow. He folded his arms. "I have led our armies across every border province, Mother. I know the land."

"You know the land where your sword has fallen," she replied, taking a slow step toward him. The heavy silk of her sari rustled against the flagstones. "You do not know the shadow that sits beside her throne. That kingdom has enemies you cannot see."

The Prince let out a short, quiet breath—half-smile, half-dismissal. 

"Every kingdom has enemies, Mother. And every enemy on our borders knows what happens when they cross our line. I am no longer a student in need of regent protection."

He bowed his head—a formal, dutiful gesture that carried no real surrender—and turned toward the carved oak doors.

The Rajmata remained motionless beside the map table. She did not call him back. She did not raise her voice.

As the doors shut behind him, her fingers slid into the folds of her sleeve, gripping the cold brass seal of her private intelligence network.

"Then you leave me no choice," she whispered to the empty room.
