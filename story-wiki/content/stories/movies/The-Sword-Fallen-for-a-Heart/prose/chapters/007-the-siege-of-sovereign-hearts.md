## 📜 Chapter Prose

Smoke hung thick inside the command tent, stinging the eyes with the sharp reek of burning pitch and scorched pine. Outside, the thunder of siege engines shook the timber foundations of the mountain fort.

The Princess stood over the tactical sand-table, her sleeve rolled up, revealing a dark bruise across her forearm where a stray catapult splinter had struck her armor. She held a red-tipped marker stone, slamming it down over the western ridge.

"If the lower pass falls, collapse the stone archways," she commanded, her voice steady and ringing with absolute authority. "We do not cede the valley to their cavalry while the infantry can still hold the palisade."

"My Lady," her veteran captain gasped, wiping mud and blood from his forehead. "Our archers are down to three quivers per man. The western kingdom... the Prince... his banners are nowhere on the horizon. He is not coming."

The Princess did not flinch. Her hand did not tremble on the marker stone. 

She turned slowly toward the open flap of the tent. Far across the valley, where the mountain road wound toward the Prince's realm, the horizon remained empty under the gray storm clouds.

"Then we stop looking at the horizon," she said, her voice dropping into cold, razor-sharp focus. "We are the only defense this kingdom has."

The tent flap opened. An envoy from the Political Ally stepped inside, escorted by two armed guards. He wore armor untouched by blood, carrying a sealed parchment tied with golden cord.

"Princess," the envoy bowed with mocking courtliness. "My lord offers an end to this destruction. A treaty of sovereign union. A marriage alliance between our houses. Sign, and our engines cease fire within the hour. Refuse, and tomorrow sunrise sees the breach of your inner citadel."

The captains drew their daggers, but the Princess raised one blood-stained hand, halting them instantly.

She stepped up to the envoy, her eyes burning with fierce, unyielding resolve. She looked at the sealed treaty, then out at her burning valley, and finally at the empty road to the west.

She reached out and took the parchment.

"I will read your terms," she said, her voice flat and unbreakable. "Not for love. For my people."
