## 📜 Chapter Prose

Incense smoke, thick with sandalwood and crushed marigolds, drifted across the marble pavilion. Sitar strings hummed in a complex evening raga, lost beneath the roar of laughter and toasts from nobles draped in heavy silks.

The Prince sat upon the elevated low bench, his fingers absently turning a silver wine goblet. Across the hall, seated behind the latticed teak screen of the royal balcony, sat the Rajmata. Her eyes never left him.

A visiting nobleman from the eastern border marches leaned across the low dining table, spilling a drop of spiced wine onto the carpet. 

"...and the wedding arrangements for the Eastern Houses remain stalled," the nobleman chuckled, motioning toward a delegation of visiting envoys. "Their sovereign keeps the heir guarded closer than a treasury vault. Ever since she returned from her childhood exile in the western villages, no suitor has gained an audience."

The Prince paused, the goblet halfway to his lips. "Exile?"

"A protective concealment, my Lord," the courtier replied, lowering his voice. "Concealed as a commoner in the border forest near the old Gurukul until she came of age. They call her the Hidden Princess."

A sharp, sudden tightness closed around the Prince's throat. The rim of the silver goblet pressed cold against his thumb.

The memory of river reeds and a birch branch flicking through humid air surged back—sharp, vivid, and blinding. Not a common girl. Not an impossible match. A princess of sovereign blood.

His pulse hammered against his ribs like a war drum.

He set the goblet down with a metallic clink that cut through the surrounding conversation. His chair scraped back across the polished marble as he stood up.

Across the room, behind the carved wooden screen, a silhouette stiffened. The Rajmata's fan stopped mid-stroke. Her gaze locked onto her son's face, catching the unmistakable spark of recognition and sudden, dangerous intent.

The Prince turned to his chief captain. "Prepare my escort for tomorrow's first light. We ride east."
