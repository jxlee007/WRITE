## 📜 Chapter Prose

The afternoon heat hung thick over the riverbank, heavy with the scent of crushed river reeds and wet clay. Sunlight filtered through the dense banyan canopy, mottling the earth where silver birch practice swords lay stacked against a stone altar.

A pebble skipped across the water, breaking the mirror surface into three wide, shimmering rings.

"Your wrist dropped on the third stroke," a voice called out from the bank above. 

The Prince turned. A girl in a simple coarse cotton tunic sat perched on a low-hanging root, her ankles swinging above the current. Mud stained her hem, but her eyes held a calm, piercing stillness that made the wooden sword feel clumsy in his grip.

"The form calls for speed, not weight," the Prince said, wiping sweat from his forehead with the back of his forearm. The rough weave of his student robe scratched against his neck.

"The form calls for balance," she countered, sliding down the embankment without a sound. She picked up a fallen birch branch, testing its weight with a swift wrist flick that whistled through the humid air. "If you strike with pride, a lighter blade will strip it from your hand before you reach the turn."

He stepped forward, bringing his wooden blade up to guard. The tip trembled slightly—not from exhaustion, but from the sudden, sharp focus her presence demanded. 

For two years in this isolated sanctuary, there were no ministers, no royal edicts, no mother's watchful couriers waiting at the gate. There was only the sound of water against river stones and the rhythmic click of wood against wood.

Their wooden blades clashed once, twice, three times—a tight, breathless cadence that ended with her branch resting inches from his throat.

Her chest rose and fell in quick, quiet breaths. Neither spoke. The river rushed past below.

A horn sounded from the northern ridge—the three long, low blasts signaling the arrival of the royal escort from the capital. His studies were over. The carriage waited.

He lowered his weapon slowly. "Will you be here when the dry season ends?"

She lowered the branch, her eyes dropping to the river reeds. "The water never stays in the same bed twice, Prince." She turned toward the village trail before he could step across the stones, her cotton skirt disappearing into the tall grass without a backward glance.
